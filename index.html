01 / THE LEGAL POSITION

Toilet CCTV is an
exceptional case.

The purpose of this website is to explain the assessment a controller must make—not to provide a shortcut to permission.

!
There is no automatic right to install CCTV in a toilet.

A genuine safety concern, a camera aimed at an entrance, warning signs, restricted access or a DPC consultation do not, individually or together, create a blanket exemption from data-protection law.

A

High expectation of privacy

People using toilets have a particularly strong expectation of privacy. The DPC specifically warns against CCTV covering cubicles and identifies a very high threshold for restroom CCTV generally.

Read the DPC restroom guidance ↗
B

Necessity is more than usefulness

A controller must demonstrate why CCTV is necessary for a defined purpose, not merely helpful. Less intrusive options must be genuinely examined and their effectiveness recorded.

Read the DPC controller guidance ↗
C

Each site is different

There is no universal camera position, technical configuration or standard form that guarantees lawfulness. The actual facts, field of view, people affected and residual risks matter.

Read the GDPR ↗
i

Scope: This guide focuses on private-sector venues open to customers or the public—such as pubs, restaurants, nightclubs, shops, leisure venues, hotels and other premises. The GDPR applies to identifiable video footage; other laws and duties may also apply depending on the setting.

02 / BEFORE ANY INSTALLATION

The legal pathway.
Step by step.

Work through these stages before any recording begins. If the assessment does not support the proposal, the answer is not to proceed.

01

Define the exact harm and purpose

Describe the specific safety or security problem. Avoid vague aims such as “general security”. Identify the location, type of incident, seriousness, frequency, dates, times and people potentially affected.

OUTPUT · WRITTEN PURPOSE STATEMENT
02

Build an evidence record

Keep dated incident logs, complaints, witness accounts, damage reports and Garda incident references where available. Separate verified facts from allegations. Restrict access to sensitive incident records.

OUTPUT · INCIDENT REGISTER + SUPPORTING EVIDENCE
03

Test less intrusive alternatives

Assess staffed supervision, attendants, patrols, access management, lighting, emergency call points, layout changes, staff training and cameras outside the restroom. Record what was tried, when, and with what result.

OUTPUT · ALTERNATIVES MATRIX
04

Ask whether internal CCTV is necessary

Can the same purpose be achieved without recording inside the facility? Would monitoring the corridor or entrance, a staffed presence or another intervention work with less intrusion? If a less intrusive effective option exists, that weighs strongly against internal CCTV.

OUTPUT · NECESSITY ANALYSIS
05

Identify and test the lawful basis

For a private operator, Article 6(1)(f) GDPR may be relevant where the controller can demonstrate a legitimate interest, necessity and a balancing of that interest against the rights and reasonable expectations of people recorded. Consent is rarely an appropriate CCTV basis in ordinary business settings.

OUTPUT · LEGITIMATE INTEREST ASSESSMENT, IF APPLICABLE
06

Complete a DPIA before processing

Describe the processing, purposes, data flows, scope, people affected, necessity and proportionality. Identify risks to customers, staff, children and vulnerable people. Specify safeguards and assess residual risk. The controller remains accountable even if a consultant prepares the DPIA.

OUTPUT · SIGNED DPIA + ACTION PLAN
07

Consult people affected and relevant staff

Consider the views of workers and users of the facilities where appropriate. Explain the proposal transparently and document feedback, concerns and the controller's response. Consultation does not replace the legal assessment.

OUTPUT · CONSULTATION RECORD
08

Apply Article 36 where residual high risk remains

If the DPIA indicates high residual risk that the controller cannot sufficiently mitigate, prior consultation with the DPC is mandatory before processing. Submit the DPIA and required supporting material. Article 36 is a statutory consultation process—not a general CCTV licence, blanket approval or permanent immunity.

OUTPUT · DPC SUBMISSION + COMPLETE WRITTEN RESPONSE, IF REQUIRED
09

Obtain independent legal and technical review

Ask an Irish data-protection solicitor and suitably qualified privacy professional to review the real proposal. Have the installer document the actual field of view, reflections, configuration, storage, access and deletion. A drawing alone cannot prove what a live camera captures.

OUTPUT · LEGAL OPINION + TECHNICAL VERIFICATION
10

Record a reasoned go / no-go decision

Proceed only if the controller's evidence supports lawfulness and proportionality, all regulatory advice and conditions are addressed, and residual risks are acceptable. Record the decision-maker, reasons, restrictions, review date and circumstances requiring suspension.

OUTPUT · SIGNED DECISION + REVIEW SCHEDULE
↗
Article 36 is not an approval certificate.

The DPC issues written advice in the statutory prior-consultation process and may also exercise its investigative, corrective or enforcement powers. Read the response carefully and comply with any advice or requirements. A controller remains responsible for ongoing GDPR compliance.

03 / PAPERWORK LIBRARY

Build the file.
Keep the evidence.

A practical document inventory for the controller. These are suggested working records—not official DPC forms, certificates or a guarantee of compliance.

01 Purpose & evidence

  • Written purpose and scope statement
  • Incident register and supporting records
  • Garda reference details, where applicable
  • Alternatives assessment and trial outcomes
  • Necessity and proportionality analysis

02 Data protection assessments

  • Legitimate Interest Assessment, if applicable
  • Data Protection Impact Assessment
  • Data-flow and roles map
  • DPO advice and management response
  • Consultation and risk-treatment records

03 Technical & operational controls

  • Floor plan and actual field-of-view diagrams
  • On-site photographs and reflection tests
  • Privacy masking and configuration records
  • Access permissions and audit logs
  • Retention schedule and deletion test log
  • Processor contracts and security controls

04 Transparency & governance

  • Short-form CCTV signs before entry
  • Full CCTV privacy notice
  • Data subject request procedure
  • Incident disclosure and breach procedure
  • Article 36 submission and response, if applicable
  • Independent legal opinion and review schedule

04 / TECHNICAL SAFEGUARDS

Reduce intrusion.
Prove the controls.

Safeguards can reduce risk. They cannot cure an unjustified location or make disproportionate surveillance lawful.

07 / COMMON QUESTIONS

Clear answers.
No false guarantees.

Does putting a camera in a corner and pointing it at the entrance make it lawful?

No automatic rule says that. The real field of view, reflections, necessity, proportionality, lawful basis, risks and DPC guidance all matter. A camera physically inside a toilet remains highly intrusive even if its view is restricted.

Do warning signs or customer consent make it lawful?

No. Signs support transparency but do not create a lawful basis or establish necessity. Consent is rarely an appropriate basis for CCTV in an ordinary business environment, especially where people may have no realistic alternative to using the facility.

Does Article 36 consultation mean the DPC has licensed the cameras?

No. Article 36 is prior consultation where a DPIA indicates high residual risk that cannot be sufficiently mitigated. The DPC issues written advice and may exercise its powers. It is not a general CCTV licensing scheme or blanket approval for future processing.

Can every business use this guide as its compliance certificate?

No. The controller is responsible for its own facts, evidence, assessment and decisions. This website is educational material only and is not a legal opinion, DPIA, licence or certificate.

What if the purpose is to prevent assault, drug dealing or harassment?

Those can be serious legitimate safety concerns. The controller must still evidence the problem, assess less intrusive measures, demonstrate necessity and proportionality, and assess the heightened privacy rights of people using the restroom. The seriousness of a problem does not automatically make every surveillance method lawful.
